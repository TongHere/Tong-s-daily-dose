## My perspective

Contracts are a system of record, acting as documents for recording obligations. They serve as a foundation for trust and fairness, bridging relationships between parties. They also represent the values and complexities of society.

A lawyer acts as an advocate for people's rights and fairness. Furthermore, the most important aspect is trustworthiness; without it, trade would never happen, which would create problems and losses for both parties.

After 4,000 years, the core value and functionality of both lawyers and contracts remain the same, revealing that no matter how technology changes, their core value and purpose remain unchanged.

Nowadays, AI is rapidly transforming the legal world; from my perspective, it streamlines long-tail legal tasks, but the core values of the legal profession remain unchanged.

## The earliest contracts

The earliest contracts date back 4,000 years ago in Mesopotamia, written on cuneiform tablets using a logical symbolic system.

They corresponded with clay tokens, which were the earliest method for documenting trade records. The clay tokens were paired with cuneiform tablets to clearly display and present the terms of the contract and trade.


---

## Purpose

The scope of contracts was broad, covering marriage, trade, and property.

Contracts serve as documents to finalize negotiations, establishing obligations and mutual agreements. They create trust between two parties, ensuring trade proceeds as intended.

Contracts also reflected social hierarchies, illustrating the complexities of society.

The first systematic legal code to be published was called the **Code of Hammurabi** — the longest and best-organized legal text from the ancient Near East. It covered property, land, commerce, marriage, family, and slaves. All of these rules were designed to manage risk.

It highlights the social hierarchies and complexities of Babylonian society.

Over time, scholars gradually evolved to study and interpret the law, though they were not yet considered lawyers.

---

## From papyrus to Rome

Gradually, as papermaking techniques changed and papyrus was introduced, contracts began to be written on papyrus, allowing them to be widely distributed.

At the same time, in ancient Rome, people had to plead their own cases in court. They would ask orators for assistance, as orators were trustworthy and skilled in persuasion. However, under the rules of that time, orators were not allowed to charge for their services.

Moreover, while orators excelled at public speaking, another group of people knowledgeable about the rules served as legal advisors behind the scenes, offering guidance to those seeking advice.

---

## The first true lawyers

The first true lawyers emerged in the late 11th century, following 600 years of the Dark Ages — a period during which legal professions were absent after the fall of the Roman Empire.

In the late 11th century, the *Corpus Juris Civilis* (The Body of Civil Law) was rediscovered — a massive, complex legal code dating back to the Byzantine Empire.

To study this code, the University of Bologna was established. After studying the law, graduates became certified experts, kicking off the modern legal profession.
