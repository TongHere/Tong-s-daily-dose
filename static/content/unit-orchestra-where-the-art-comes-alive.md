## My perspective

From my perspective, using the Mighty Wurlitzer to perform - this concept is very similar to how AI generates music today. People use prompts to create an entire piece of music. However, the difference I see is that with the Mighty Wurlitzer, or any other music performances, musicians actively participat in performing, experiencing and engaging with it through their performance and the music itself. This process is very different than writing a prompt to generate the music.

With AI, humans participate mainly through writing — which, from my perspective, raises the question: should poets or writers be the ones crafting prompts to create the AI generated music instead?

## The Mighty Wurlitzer

I went to the Museum of Musical Instruments today and saw the Mighty Wurlitzer for the first time — a theatre organ, which provided background music for silent movies. It could create the sounds of a whole orchestra: strings, percussion, woodwinds, brass, tubular chimes, piano, rolling thunder, birdsong whistles, gunshots, and telephone rings.

At the Mighty Wurlitzer, an organist played keyboards containing four main manuals (keyboards), along with three rows of stop tabs. When played, it triggered the pipes to produce sound.

The entire pipeline is huge, occupying around three rooms, with all the components connected to each other.

Music itself feels vibrant and full of joy, creating a positive and uplifting atmosphere for the audience as well.
