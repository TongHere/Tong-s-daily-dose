# Overcoming the Incentive Collapse Paradox

**Date:** May 25, 2026

**Paper:** Qichuan Yin, Ziwei Su, Shuangning Li. [Overcoming the Incentive Collapse Paradox](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27049). *arXiv:2603.27049* (2026).

---

Key takeaway :
1. Human - AI contracting paradox - When AI getting better and  With AI errors becoming increasingly rare, human compensation for error detection and verification should be inversely proportional to the probability of an AI error occurring.

While humans are rewarded for catching AI errors, a high AI accuracy rate means workers must expend significant effort to find fewer mistakes. Consequently, the compensation no longer matches the high level of effort required. 

Then this system formalize the incentive collapse phenomenon.

2. In this paper, they design the mechanism called sentinel-auditing payment scheme. By deliberating selecting task where the AI is unreliable or constructed some tasks to be difficult for AI. 

3. This paper combine conomic (contract theory) sentinel mechanism to correct human shirking behavior, while simultaneously employing advanced statistics (M-estimation and asymptotic theory) to ensure that the rectified data maintains statistically sound.

Reading note - From the Article :
Problem - 
1. AI tools are rapidly becoming embedded in everyday work
and professional workflow

2. These tools substantially improve efficiency and reduce cognitive load.

3.  However, recent work by Bastani & Cachon (2025) formalizes
what they term the human-AI contracting paradox: as
AI accuracy improves, sustaining nonzero human effort
through accuracy-based incentives becomes increasingly
expensive. When AI errors are rare, a rational human agent
can free-ride on the AI with minimal effort, and preventing
this behavior requires payments that scale inversely with
the AI error probability. As a result, accuracy-based payment schemes can collapse precisely when AI becomes most
reliable.
