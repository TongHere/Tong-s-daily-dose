I've found that the way people use AI is so different.

Some people use AI in a very cautious way.

Some people use AI with guardrails.

Some people use AI without thinking at all.

I read a paper talking about how, as AI becomes better and better, people will lose the incentive to put effort into checking if it is correct or not.

I feel more like the concept of "better and better"—and the standard for what makes it better—differs from person to person.

This is very interesting because, with the exact same answer from an AI, some people might think it is not enough, some might just think, "Why not?", and others might just think, "I don't care."

It's the same answer from the AI, but it results in completely different behaviors from people. I guess who acts as the guardrail for the AI's output is also important. The meaning of that threshold will be different for everyone.

During my workday today, I saw that many colleagues, as well as myself, are relying on AI to write documentation.

I think people use AI to create documentation. In this way, AI should act as an assistant to complement human work. AI creates structure and helps PMs find perspectives they haven't thought about. It helps organize notes and knowledge, but the core content is still based on us.

AI can do so many things in one day, but what is the most valuable thing? Living your day-to-day life and continuing to work on what you love the most. That is something AI can't do. That is something only you can do, because it is your life.
